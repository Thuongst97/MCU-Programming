From the STM32G474 to Automotive-Grade MCUs

The NUCLEO-G474RE taught you the concepts with an affordable chip you can see every register of. Real ECUs use microcontrollers built and qualified for cars. This appendix explains what is different about them and maps each lesson of the course onto three families you are likely to meet: NXP S32K, Infineon AURIX and Renesas RH850.

C.1Why the G474 isn't automotive

The STM32G474RET6 is a general-purpose industrial part. It has some features an automotive designer likes (CAN FD, flash ECC, a capable FPU), but it lacks what a car maker requires from a qualified part:

QualificationAEC-Q100 stress tests, grade by ambient temperature (grade 1: −40 to 125 °C, grade 0: up to 150 °C)industrial range, −40 to 85 °C for the "6" suffix (DS12288, ordering information)

CommunicationCAN FD, several CAN channels, LIN, often Ethernet and FlexRaythree FDCAN controllers (CAN FD) and USART LIN mode; no Ethernet or FlexRay

Analogy

The NUCLEO is a learner car on an empty parking lot: every control is visible and nothing bad happens if you stall. An automotive MCU is the same car built for the motorway in winter: same pedals, plus airbags, ABS, a service history and a manufacturer who promises spare parts for fifteen years.

⚠️
Never in a real vehicle

Nothing from this course, hardware or software, belongs in a vehicle on a public road. The capstone imitates an ECU to teach how one is built; it is not qualified, not safety-analysed and not tested to automotive standards.

C.3Vendor MCAL and tools

On these parts you rarely write registers for production. The silicon vendor delivers an AUTOSAR MCAL (NXP: Real-Time Drivers; Infineon: AURIX MCAL; Renesas: RH850 MCAL), configured with a tool such as EB tresos or the vendor's own configurator, plus low-level drivers for non-AUTOSAR projects. The modules you built in autosar/mcal (Port, Dio, Pwm, Adc, Can, Wdg, Mcu) are exactly the ones those packages contain, with many more options.

  • Evaluation boards (for example S32K144EVB, AURIX lite kits, RH850 starter kits) cost about as much as a good multimeter and come with debuggers on board.
  • Toolchains: GCC works for Cortex-M parts; TriCore and RH850 usually use vendor or commercial compilers (some with free versions for evaluation).
  • What transfers directly: clocks and PLLs, wait states, NVIC-style interrupt priorities, timers and PWM, ADC with DMA, CAN bit timing, watchdogs, startup code and linker scripts, MISRA, and everything AUTOSAR.
