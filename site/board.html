A.1Interactive pinout

Click a pin to see its default functions, the functions it gains after an AFIO remap, its LQFP48 pin number and the examples that use it. Type a function into the search box (CAN, ADC, USART2) to find every pin that offers it. Switch to 5 V tolerance to see which pins survive a 5 V signal.

⚠️
3.3 V only means 3.3 V only

Pins marked "3.3 V only" (all of PA0–PA7, PB0, PB1, PB5 and PC13–PC15) are not 5 V tolerant (DS5319 Table 5, I/O level column). A 5 V signal on them can damage the chip. The analog-capable pins are exactly the ones that are not tolerant.

Pin functions come from the datasheet (DS5319 Table 5) and the remap tables in RM0008 §9.3. The header order is the common Blue Pill layout from community schematics: on most boards it matches; check the silkscreen on yours.

🎭
Analogy

The BOOT jumpers are like the boot menu key on a PC: they decide which "disk" the chip starts from. Normally it starts from its own flash (your program). Set BOOT0 and it starts from a small ROM program from ST that can receive a new firmware over the serial port.

A.3Known quirks

Clone and counterfeit MCUs

Many Blue Pills carry a chip that is not made by ST: CKS32F103, CS32F103, APM32F103 or GD32F103. They are software-compatible for most of this course, but not identical. On most boards the first sign is the debugger: the debug port reports ID 0x2ba01477 instead of ST's 0x1ba01477, and OpenOCD refuses to continue. Use examples/common/openocd/bluepill-clone.cfg. Other differences (timing of the flash, ADC accuracy, maximum clock) vary by maker; check yours.

64 KB or 128 KB?

The C8 is specified with 64 KB of flash; the CB with 128 KB. Many C8 chips contain 128 KB and report it in the flash size register at 0x1FFFF7E0 (RM0008 §30), and OpenOCD's stock Blue Pill board file even assumes 128 KB. The upper 64 KB is not guaranteed to work, so the course never uses it: our linker script and OpenOCD configuration both say 64 KB, and the build fails if a program grows past it.

SWD can be switched off by your own program

PA13 (SWDIO) and PA14 (SWCLK) are debug pins after reset. The SWJ_CFG field of AFIO_MAPR can release them for other uses: JTAGDISABLE (010) frees only the JTAG pins PA15, PB3 and PB4 and keeps SWD working; DISABLE (100) frees PA13/PA14 as well, and the debugger can no longer connect. ⚠️
Never write SWJ_CFG = DISABLE

Use AFIO_MAPR_SWJ_CFG_JTAGDISABLE when you need PA15, PB3 or PB4. If you have already locked yourself out, recover with BOOT0 = 1.

Other things to know

  • PC13–PC15 are weak. They sink about 3 mA, switch at 2 MHz at most and must not source current (DS5319 Table 5 notes).
  • USB and CAN cannot run together. They share 512 bytes of packet SRAM and interrupt vectors 19 and 20 (RM0008 §24 and §23).
  • Errata. ST lists known silicon limitations in errata sheet ES096; the I2C peripheral in particular needs care. Modules mention the items that affect them.
