ADC and DMA

The ADC turns a voltage on a pin into a 12-bit number. DMA then moves those numbers into memory without the CPU touching every one. Together they are how real firmware samples sensors continuously: the CPU only looks at finished, averaged results.

⏱ 45 min read🎯 Intermediate📂 examples/05-adc-pot-dma

9.1How a SAR ADC works

The STM32G474 has five 12-bit successive-approximation (SAR) ADCs (DS12288). A SAR ADC finds the input voltage by binary search: a sample-and-hold capacitor freezes the input, then for each bit from the most significant down, an internal DAC produces a trial voltage and a comparator says "higher" or "lower". Twelve comparisons give twelve bits. Move the slider:

A 12-bit conversion takes the sampling time you choose plus 12.5 ADC clock cycles (RM0440, ADC). The course clocks ADC1 synchronously from HCLK ÷ 4 = 42.5 MHz (ADC12_COMMON->CCR.CKMODE = 11), so even the shortest sampling time (2.5 cycles) gives about 0.35 µs per conversion; DS12288 gives the maximum ADC clock and sampling rate.

🎭
Analogy

Guessing a number between 0 and 4095 with "higher or lower" answers: start at 2048, then 1024 or 3072, and so on. Twelve questions always find it. The sampling time is how long you let a photograph develop before you start asking: too short and the picture is blurred.

9.2Bringing up ADC1

The G4 ADC wakes up in deep power-down and must be brought up in a fixed order (RM0440, ADC): leave deep power-down (DEEPPWD = 0), start its internal voltage regulator (ADVREGEN = 1) and wait for it, calibrate (ADCAL), then enable (ADEN) and wait for ADRDY. Skip or reorder a step and you get no conversions or wrong ones, with no error flag to tell you why.

⚠️
0 V to VREF+, never more

An input in analog mode must stay between 0 V and VREF+ (3.3 V on the NUCLEO, where VREF+ follows VDDA). A pin that is 5 V tolerant as a digital input is not in analog mode. The pinout lists each pin's ADC channels; PA1 is ADC1_IN2.

9.4DMA and DMAMUX

The DMA controller is a second bus master that copies data between peripherals and memory. The G474 has two of them, DMA1 and DMA2, with 8 channels each. On the G4 no peripheral is wired to a fixed channel: the DMAMUX sits in between and forwards the request you choose to the channel you choose (RM0440, DMA and DMAMUX). DMAMUX channel 0 feeds DMA1 channel 1, DMAMUX channel 1 feeds DMA1 channel 2, and so on. The request numbers (ADC1 = 5, SPI2_RX = 12, …) are listed in the DMAMUX chapter of RM0440. Per DMA channel you set:

  • CPAR: the peripheral address (for example &ADC1->DR),
  • CMAR: the memory address (your buffer),
  • CNDTR: how many items to move,
  • CCR: direction, sizes, increments, circular mode, priority, interrupts, enable.
⚠️
Two classic silent failures

No DMAMUX request: the DMA channel is enabled, configured perfectly, and never moves a byte, because nothing asks it to. Buffer in CCM SRAM: the DMA controllers cannot reach the 32 KB CCM SRAM at 0x10000000, so a buffer placed there never fills. Keep DMA buffers in SRAM1/SRAM2, which is where the course's linker script puts every ordinary variable.

9.5Scan + DMA circular

The ADC converts a sequence of channels (ADC_SQR1–SQR4, length in SQR1.L); in continuous mode (CFGR.CONT) it starts over by itself; with CFGR.DMAEN it asks for a DMA transfer after each conversion, and DMACFG = 1 keeps asking forever, for a circular DMA buffer. With DMA in circular mode the buffer fills forever, and the CPU just reads it when it wants a value.

💡
Half-transfer: double buffering for free

Enable HTIE and TCIE: the half-transfer interrupt says "the first half is complete, process it while I fill the second", and transfer-complete says the opposite. Your code never reads a half that DMA is writing.

9.6Noise, averaging and Vrefint

Real readings jitter by a few counts. Three cheap fixes, in order of effort:

  1. Longer sampling time for high-impedance sources, so the sampling capacitor charges fully.
  2. Averaging: the mean of N samples reduces random noise by about √N. Example 05 averages 32 samples from its DMA buffer.
  3. Hardware: a 100 nF capacitor from the ADC pin to GND, short wires, and a quiet supply.

The internal temperature sensor (ADC1 channel 16, enabled with ADC12_COMMON->CCR.VSENSESEL) comes with two factory calibration points stored in system memory, taken at 30 °C and 130 °C (TEMPSENSOR_CAL1/2 in ST's LL driver). Interpolating between them gives a usable absolute die temperature, which is still not the air temperature around the board.

⚠️
The supply is your reference

Every reading is relative to VDDA. If the 3.3 V rail sags while a radio transmits, all readings rise, even though the sensor did not change. Measuring Vrefint alongside, as example 05 does, corrects for that.
