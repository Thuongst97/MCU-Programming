ADC and DMA

The ADC turns a voltage on a pin into a 12-bit number. DMA then moves those numbers into memory without the CPU touching every one. Together they are how real firmware samples sensors continuously: the CPU only looks at finished, averaged results.

⏱ 45 min read🎯 Intermediate📂 examples/05-adc-pot-dma

9.1How a SAR ADC works

The STM32F103 has two 12-bit successive-approximation (SAR) ADCs (DS5319). A SAR ADC finds the input voltage by binary search: a sample-and-hold capacitor freezes the input, then for each bit from the most significant down, an internal DAC produces a trial voltage and a comparator says "higher" or "lower". Twelve comparisons give twelve bits. Move the slider:

A conversion takes the sampling time you choose plus 12.5 ADC clock cycles. At the maximum ADC clock of 14 MHz and the shortest sampling time (1.5 cycles), that is 1 µs per conversion (RM0008 §11.6). The Blue Pill's 72 MHz setup gives 12 MHz (PCLK2 ÷ 6).

🎭
Analogy

Guessing a number between 0 and 4095 with "higher or lower" answers: start at 2048, then 1024 or 3072, and so on. Twelve questions always find it. The sampling time is how long you let a photograph develop before you start asking: too short and the picture is blurred.

Setting up ADC1 takes four things: a clock within limits, an analog pin, a sampling time, and a calibration.

⚠️
ADC pins are not 5 V tolerant

PA0–PA7, PB0 and PB1 carry the ADC inputs and none of them tolerates 5 V. The input range is 0 V to VREF+, which is 3.3 V on the Blue Pill (VREF+ is tied to VDDA inside the 48-pin package).

9.4DMA basics

The DMA controller is a second bus master that copies data between peripherals and memory. DMA1 has 7 channels; each peripheral request is wired to a fixed channel (RM0008 §13.3.7): ADC1 uses channel 1, SPI1 RX and TX channels 2 and 3, USART1 TX and RX channels 4 and 5, and so on. Per channel you set:

  • CPAR: the peripheral address (for example &ADC1->DR),
  • CMAR: the memory address (your buffer),
  • CNDTR: how many items to move,
  • CCR: direction, sizes, increments, circular mode, priority, interrupts, enable.

9.5Scan + DMA circular

In scan mode the ADC converts a list of channels in sequence (SQR1–SQR3); in continuous mode it starts over by itself; with ADC_CR2.DMA set it asks for a DMA transfer after each conversion. With DMA in circular mode the buffer fills forever, and the CPU just reads it when it wants a value.

💡
Half-transfer: double buffering for free

Enable HTIE and TCIE: the half-transfer interrupt says "the first half is complete, process it while I fill the second", and transfer-complete says the opposite. Your code never reads a half that DMA is writing.

9.6Noise, averaging and Vrefint

Real readings jitter by a few counts. Three cheap fixes, in order of effort:

  1. Longer sampling time for high-impedance sources, so the sampling capacitor charges fully.
  2. Averaging: the mean of N samples reduces random noise by about √N. Example 05 averages 32 samples from its DMA buffer.
  3. Hardware: a 100 nF capacitor from the ADC pin to GND, short wires, and a quiet supply.

The internal temperature sensor (channel 16, enabled with TSVREFE) changes about 4.3 mV per °C, but its offset varies so much from chip to chip that it is only useful for detecting temperature changes, not for absolute measurements (RM0008 §11.10).

⚠️
The supply is your reference

Every reading is relative to VDDA. If the 3.3 V rail sags while a radio transmits, all readings rise, even though the sensor did not change. Measuring Vrefint alongside, as example 05 does, corrects for that.
