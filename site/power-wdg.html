Module 13 · Peripherals

Watchdogs, Reset Sources and Low Power

Two things a product needs and a demo never shows: recovering by itself when the software hangs, and spending most of its life asleep. This module covers the STM32G474's two watchdogs, how to find out why the chip reset, the Sleep, Stop, Standby and Shutdown modes, and the surprises waiting after a wake-up.

⏱ 45 min read🎯 Intermediate📂 examples/08-iwdg-hardfault, examples/09-lowpower-stop

13.1Who reset me?

Every reset sets a flag in RCC_CSR (RM0440, RCC chapter, "Control/status register"), and the flags stay set until software clears them with RMVF. Reading them at boot is the cheapest diagnostic a device can have: a field unit that keeps rebooting because of the watchdog tells you exactly that.

ℹ️
PINRSTF is almost always set, BORRSTF means power-on too

Every internal reset source also drives the NRST pin low for a moment, so the pin flag accompanies all the others. And the G4 has no separate power-on flag: a power-on sets BORRSTF. Check the specific flags first and treat "pin" as the fallback, as the decoder above does.

13.2Independent watchdog

The independent watchdog (IWDG) is a 12-bit down-counter clocked by the ~32 kHz LSI, independent of the main clock. If software does not reload it in time, it resets the chip. Once started it cannot be stopped, not even by the program that started it (RM0440, IWDG chapter). The G4's IWDG also has an optional window register (WINR); example 08 leaves it at its maximum, so the window is off.

Pick a prescaler and reload value. The LSI frequency is not trimmed and varies from chip to chip and with temperature (DS12288, LSI characteristics), so the real timeout varies too: design for the short end.

⚠️
Feed the watchdog from the right place

Kicking the watchdog from a timer interrupt keeps the chip alive even when the main loop is stuck, which defeats the purpose. Feed it only when every important activity has proved it is running (the capstone's watchdog manager does exactly that).

🎭
Analogy

A dead man's switch on a train: the driver must press a pedal every few seconds. If the driver falls asleep, the train stops by itself. Pressing the pedal with a brick (a timer interrupt) defeats it.

13.3Window watchdog

The window watchdog (WWDG) runs from PCLK1 and catches a second class of bug: refreshing too early. Its 7-bit counter counts down; a reset happens if it drops below 0x40, or if software refreshes it while it is still above the window value W (RM0440, WWDG chapter). Refreshes are only accepted inside the window, so a loop that runs too fast, for example because it skipped work, also resets the chip.

13.4Sleep, Stop, Standby, Shutdown

The Cortex-M4 enters a low-power mode with WFI (wait for interrupt) or WFE (wait for event). With SCB->SCR.SLEEPDEEP clear you get Sleep; with it set, PWR_CR1.LPMS picks which deep mode (RM0440, PWR chapter, "Low-power modes"):

Typical currents range from milliamps in Sleep to microamps in Stop and below a microamp in Shutdown (DS12288 supply-current tables). On a NUCLEO the ST-LINK, its LEDs and the regulator draw far more than the MCU in Stop. UM2505 describes the IDD jumper (JP5 on most NUCLEO-64 boards; check yours) where you can put an ammeter in series with the MCU supply alone.

13.5Wake-up sources

Example 09 shows both: Sleep, woken by every SysTick interrupt, and Stop 1, woken only by the blue USER button B1 (PC13) on EXTI line 13.

⚠️
The lost wake-up race

If the button is pressed after you decide to sleep but before WFI executes, its interrupt runs while you are still awake, and WFI then sleeps until the next press. Example 09 accepts that (it is a demo), but products check a "pending work" flag with interrupts masked right before sleeping, or use WFE with the event register, which remembers an event that happened before the instruction.

13.6Restoring 170 MHz after Stop

Stop mode switches off the PLL. When an interrupt wakes the chip, it continues on HSI16 (RM0440, PWR chapter, "Stop 1 mode"): the UART baud rate, SysTick period and every timer are now wrong by a factor of more than ten until the clock tree is rebuilt. Rebuilding means the whole 170 MHz sequence again, including the AHB ÷2 step.

✅ Both build in CI; the fault and reset-cause decoders pass their host tests. ⚠️ The reset-flag bit positions and the Stop-mode wake-up clock come from ST's CMSIS header and HAL sources; their RM0440 sections are still to be checked (docs/verification.md). ❌ Not run on a NUCLEO-G474RE by the course yet, and no current was measured.

💡
Can't connect after flashing a low-power program?

If the chip enters Stop or Standby right after reset, the debugger may never catch it awake. Connect under reset (the ST-LINK drives NRST), as Troubleshooting shows.
