Watchdogs, Reset Sources and Low Power

Two things a product needs and a demo never shows: recovering by itself when the software hangs, and spending most of its life asleep. This module covers the watchdogs, how to find out why the chip reset, the Sleep, Stop and Standby modes, and the surprises waiting after a wake-up.

⏱ 45 min read🎯 Intermediate📂 examples/08-iwdg-hardfault, examples/09-lowpower-stop

13.1Who reset me?

Every reset sets a flag in RCC_CSR (RM0008 §7.3.10), and the flags stay set until software clears them with RMVF. Reading them at boot is the cheapest diagnostic a device can have: a field unit that keeps rebooting because of the watchdog tells you exactly that.

ℹ️
PINRSTF is almost always set

Every internal reset source also pulls the NRST pin low for a moment, so the pin flag accompanies all the others. Check the specific flags first and treat "pin" as the fallback, as the decoder above does.

13.2Independent watchdog

The independent watchdog (IWDG) is a 12-bit down-counter clocked by the ~40 kHz LSI, independent of the main clock. If software does not reload it in time, it resets the chip. Once started it cannot be stopped, not even by the program that started it (RM0008 §19).

Pick a prescaler and reload value. Because the LSI may run anywhere from 30 to 60 kHz (DS5319), the real timeout varies by a factor of two: design for the short end.

⚠️
Feed the watchdog from the right place

Kicking the watchdog from a timer interrupt keeps the chip alive even when the main loop is stuck, which defeats the purpose. Feed it only when every important activity has proved it is running (the capstone's watchdog manager does exactly that).

🎭
Analogy

A dead man's switch on a train: the driver must press a pedal every few seconds. If the driver falls asleep, the train stops by itself. Pressing the pedal with a brick (a timer interrupt) defeats it.

13.3Window watchdog

The window watchdog (WWDG) runs from PCLK1 and catches a second class of bug: refreshing too early. Its 7-bit counter counts down; a reset happens if it drops below 0x40, or if software refreshes it while it is still above the window value W (RM0008 §20). Refreshes are only accepted inside the window, so a loop that runs too fast, for example because it skipped work, also resets the chip.

13.4Sleep, Stop, Standby

The Cortex-M3 enters a low-power mode with WFI (wait for interrupt) or WFE (wait for event). What stops depends on SCB->SCR.SLEEPDEEP and PWR_CR (RM0008 §5.3):

ModeStopsWake-upKeeps
SleepCPU clock onlyany interrupt; fasteverything
Stopall clocks of the 1.8 V domain, PLL, HSI, HSEany EXTI line (pins, RTC alarm, PVD, USB wake-up); microseconds, on HSISRAM and registers
Standby1.8 V domain powered offWKUP pin (PA0) rising, RTC alarm, NRST, IWDG; like a resetbackup registers and RTC only

Typical currents range from milliamps in Sleep to tens of microamps in Stop and a few microamps in Standby (DS5319 supply-current tables). On a Blue Pill the power LED and the regulator draw more than the MCU in Stop, so measure the board, then remove the LED's resistor if you need the real numbers.

Example 09 shows both: Sleep, woken by every SysTick interrupt, and Stop, woken only by the button on EXTI line 0.

⚠️
The lost wake-up race

If the button is pressed after you decide to sleep but before WFI executes, its interrupt runs while you are still awake, and WFI then sleeps until the next press. Example 09 accepts that (it is a demo), but products check a "pending work" flag with interrupts masked right before sleeping, or use WFE with the event register, which remembers an event that happened before the instruction.

13.6Restoring clocks after Stop

Stop mode switches off the PLL and the HSE. When an interrupt wakes the chip, it continues on the 8 MHz HSI (RM0008 §5.3.5): the UART baud rate, SysTick period and every timer are now wrong by a factor of nine until the clock tree is rebuilt.

13.7Debugging low-power code

In Stop and Standby the debug logic loses its clock and the debugger disconnects. DBGMCU_CR keeps the clocks the debugger needs running in low-power modes, and can freeze the watchdogs and timers while the core is halted at a breakpoint (RM0008 §31.16.3):

Example 08 sets DBG_IWDG_STOP so a breakpoint never triggers a watchdog reset; example 09 sets DBG_SLEEP and DBG_STOP in debug builds only, because they raise the current consumption.

💡
Can't connect after flashing a low-power program?

If the chip enters Stop or Standby right after reset, the debugger may never catch it awake. Connect under reset, or boot with BOOT0 = 1 (Troubleshooting).
