Clocks: RCC, PLL, Boost Mode and Flash Wait States

Every peripheral on the chip counts time in clock cycles. Get the clock tree right and baud rates, timer periods and CAN bit times come out exactly as calculated. Get it wrong and things fail in ways that look unrelated. This module covers the STM32G4 clock system end to end, including the three extra steps that 170 MHz needs.

⏱ 40 min read🎯 Intermediate📂 examples/common/bsp/clock.c

4.1Why clocks matter

A clock is a square wave that tells digital logic when to take its next step. On the STM32G474 one clock source feeds a tree of dividers, and each branch drives a group of peripherals. Three reasons to care:

  • Speed. The core runs at up to 170 MHz, more than ten times faster than after reset.
  • Correct timing. A UART's baud rate, a timer's period and a CAN bit time are all divided down from a clock. If you assume the wrong clock, every derived rate is wrong by the same factor.
  • Power. Current consumption grows roughly with clock frequency, and every peripheral with its clock off costs (almost) nothing (DS12288, supply current tables).
🎭
Analogy

The clock tree is a gearbox. One motor (the oscillator) turns a main shaft (SYSCLK); gears (prescalers) make slower shafts for the slower machines. If you change the motor speed, every machine changes speed with it, unless you also change the gears.

⚠️
The ×2 timer rule

When an APB prescaler is not 1, the timers on that bus get twice the bus clock (RM0440, RCC). At the course setup both APB prescalers are 1, so PCLK1, PCLK2 and every timer run at 170 MHz. Change APB1 to ÷ 2 in the tool and watch TIM2–7 stay at 170 MHz while PCLK1 halves. Forgetting this halves or doubles every timer period. Module 07 builds on it.

4.4Flash wait states, caches and boost mode

Two things limit how fast the chip may run: the voltage its core logic gets, and how fast flash can deliver data.

Voltage range. The internal regulator has two ranges (PWR_CR1.VOS); range 1, the reset value, is the fast one. Range 1 itself has a normal mode (up to 150 MHz) and a boost mode (up to 170 MHz), chosen by PWR_CR5.R1MODE: 1 = normal, the reset value, 0 = boost (RM0440, PWR). The PWR block needs its clock (RCC_APB1ENR1.PWREN) before you can write it.

Flash wait states. At high clock speeds the CPU would ask for the next instruction before the flash has it, so the flash interface inserts wait states. How many depends on HCLK and on the voltage range (the limits below are the ones ST's own LL code uses):

On the G4 the latency macros are values

The STM32G4 CMSIS header defines FLASH_ACR_LATENCY_0WS to FLASH_ACR_LATENCY_15WS as field values, so FLASH_ACR_LATENCY_4WS really means four wait states. (On some older STM32 families, similarly named macros are single bits of the field, a classic trap.) Set the latency before raising the clock, and lower it only after the clock has come down. Reading it back confirms the flash interface took the new value.

⚠️
The AHB ÷ 2 step

Jumping straight from 16 MHz to 170 MHz makes the chip's current consumption rise in one step, which can dip the core supply. When the target SYSCLK is above 80 MHz, RM0440 asks for the AHB prescaler at ÷ 2 for at least 1 µs around the switch. ST's own HAL does the same: look for pllfreq > 80000000U in stm32g4xx_hal_rcc.c.

4.6Peripheral and kernel clocks

Each peripheral has an enable bit in one of the enable registers, by bus: RCC_AHB1ENR (DMA, DMAMUX, CRC, flash interface), RCC_AHB2ENR (GPIO ports, ADCs, DACs), RCC_APB1ENR1/RCC_APB1ENR2 and RCC_APB2ENR. Until its bit is set, a peripheral ignores writes, which is the most common reason for "my register writes don't do anything".

Many G4 peripherals have a second clock, the kernel clock, which times the work itself: the bits of a UART frame, an I2C clock period, a CAN time quantum. RCC_CCIPR selects it per peripheral. The bus clock only serves register access. This course leaves every kernel-clock selection at its reset value, which is the peripheral's bus clock (PCLK1/PCLK2), except FDCAN, which it explicitly sets to PCLK1 (module 12).

💡
Which bus is it on?

Look the peripheral up in the enable registers above, or in the block diagram. GPIO ports and ADCs are on AHB2; SYSCFG, USART1, SPI1 and TIM1/8/15–17/20 on APB2; LPUART1, I2C, SPI2/3, TIM2–7, FDCAN and the window watchdog on APB1.
