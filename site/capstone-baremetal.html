Module 24 · Capstone

Capstone 1: Turn-Signal ECU, Bare Metal

Time to build something that behaves like a product: a turn-signal and hazard ECU that flashes two lamps, detects a blown bulb, talks to an instrument cluster over CAN and recovers from a software hang. Stage 1 builds it bare metal from drivers you already wrote. Stages 2 and 3 rebuild the same ECU on FreeRTOS and in AUTOSAR layers, and the same host tests and hardware checklist must pass every time.

⏱ 60 min read🎯 Advanced📂 capstone/turnlight (logic, hw, stage1-baremetal, cluster)🔧 2 NUCLEO-G474RE, 3 buttons, 2 LEDs, pot, 2 × 3.3 V CAN (FD) transceivers, SSD1306

ℹ️
Hypothetical data

CAN IDs, signal layouts and DTC numbers in the capstone are made up for the course. The flash-rate range follows the direction-indicator rules of UN ECE Regulation No. 48 (90 ± 30 flashes per minute); the project target is tighter.

24.2Architecture

Two boards on one CAN bus. The ECU node reads the stalk and hazard buttons, drives two lamps with PWM, measures lamp current, and reports its state; the cluster node shows telltales on the OLED and acknowledges every status frame.

Analogy

The logic folder is the recipe; the stages are three kitchens. A home kitchen (bare metal), a restaurant with a brigade of cooks (FreeRTOS tasks) and a franchise kitchen with standard stations and forms (AUTOSAR) all cook the same dish. If the dish tastes different, the kitchen is wrong, not the recipe.

24.3Turn-signal state machine

The heart of the ECU is one state machine (logic/turnlight_sm.c). Try it: this simulator runs the same rules in your browser, 10 ms per step, and draws the last four seconds of both lamps.

⚠️
Pure logic is what makes three stages possible

The state machine takes debounced inputs and elapsed time and returns lamp commands: no registers, no clock, no OS. That is why it has host tests for every requirement, and why stages 2 and 3 can reuse it unchanged. Put one register access inside it and all of that is gone.

24.5Acceptance tests

Acceptance runs in two places, and both are the same for stage 1, 2 and 3 (capstone/turnlight/docs/acceptance.md). On the host, the Unity tests in logic/test/ drive the shared state machine, lamp monitor and message packing millisecond by millisecond, so the logic of TL-REQ-001 to 007 is checked on every push. One of them:

There is no simulator for the STM32G4, so everything that needs the chip is a manual checklist on two NUCLEO boards: flash rate with a logic analyser on PA6, the 20 ms ramp on a scope, lamp-out with the pot, the cluster on a second board, cluster loss by holding B1 on the cluster, the watchdog reset, and the reset cause after pressing B2.
